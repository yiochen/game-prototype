Original native projects are backed up byte-for-byte; all full raw ImageGen targets, exact submitted prompts and input roles/hashes are retained. All final geometry/materials render natively from editable Blender scenes. No generated target is a runtime impostor, recolored export or paint-over.

## What changed and what remains different

The armchair has a broad asymmetric back, integrated torn arm showing wooden structure, and a small stuffing tuft. The cabinet has two crooked attached doors, a clear cubby and an enclosed low body. Their much smaller masks increase object density without invisible surrounding clearances. E/W cabinet and car masks rotate with the real native model.

The rockery redesign deliberately replaced the regular slab/column geometry with uneven bulging shoulders and broad crowns. Increasing height was rejected when it made the forms more column-like. Dense pots initially hid the new asset; that was a consumer layout problem, corrected through fewer outer-lane pots and exposed rock faces rather than enlarging the model. The final default-camera edge/corner consumer was inspected in addition to a separately labeled wider pan. The scene now reuses the approved module at varied facings alongside independently positioned current plants, barrels, crates, lanterns and signs.

The native source camera exposes more top area than the generated targets; this is a required registration difference, not an error hidden by warping. Exact stain/tear/wood-grain positions and unseen side geometry are interpreted natively. Current native lighting is quieter and more even than the ImageGen scene's painterly lighting. The comparison explicitly shows that difference. The original wall/HUD/actors remain current, and target text or extra ornament does not introduce mechanics.

These approvals cover four **static prototype facings** and their declared consumers. Native selection/clearance animation, Godot commands, save migration, real arrival/staff limits and Android verification remain after UI approval. Static model approval is not user approval of the composed UI.

## Review and timing record

Armchair: one formal art inspection plus an evidence-only reseal; the sole packet blocker was a coordinator style-reference path/hash correction after sealing, not a visual defect. Cabinet followed the same art/evidence pattern. Rockery retained three inspected provisional draft reviews, then one formal complete approval. No approved image was rerendered or altered after its final verdict.

Recorded independent calibration-to-final-approval intervals were approximately 11 minutes for armchair; rockery's formal complete review was about 85 seconds. Cabinet's initial two review event timestamps were written together at verdict, so they do not establish a review-duration measurement. Overall editing/generation/queue wall time was not comprehensively instrumented; missing intervals are unknown. Source/review event ledgers are retained under each experiment. Parallel actor times are not added together as elapsed throughput.
