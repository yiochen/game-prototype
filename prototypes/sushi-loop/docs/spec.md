Sushi Loop is the working title. This spec includes the latest wireframe revisions. The React studio reviews UI; restaurant and expedition simulations remain future work.

## Problem Statement

Players want a mobile idle and incremental management game that feels alive, rewards observation and strategic choices, and works comfortably with simple touch controls. Repeatedly moving a manager to collect, cook, or serve makes restaurant games feel like chores. Generic upgrade ladders also leave little room to discover a personal strategy.

Players need a restaurant whose customers, chefs, dishes, and layout visibly explain its performance, with lasting growth and useful progress while away. Short active excursions should introduce variety and new restaurant possibilities without making idle play punitive or replacing management with mandatory manual work.

## Solution

Build a persistent, portrait-oriented sushi restaurant paired with short submarine expeditions. The restaurant operates automatically. The player observes service, changes belt routes and seating, hires and develops chefs, chooses their ongoing recipes, and clears neighboring expansion patches. Express performance through character reactions, dishes, and animation rather than an earning-rate dashboard.

The submarine recharges passively between expeditions. The player steers through an ocean-bed canyon to collect salvage, then harpoons and follows one creature near the journey's end. A completed first catch unlocks that species' restaurant recipe. Coins develop the restaurant; salvage develops the submarine. Earned rewards survive failed and deliberately shortened expeditions.

Present both activities in the accepted 2D doodle style. Keep the restaurant reassuring while idle, the expedition readable during touch play, and recipe discovery celebratory. Preserve the restaurant and unfinished expedition across sessions.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Owned game boundary.** Build one independent game with its own restaurant simulation, expedition simulation, presentation, content, balance, assets, persistence, and tests. Share repository development/build/hosting/test tooling only. Do not import gameplay from sibling games or the former life simulation. Keep the browser entry registered in the shared prototype catalog. The artifacts specify mobile portrait behavior rather than choosing a runtime framework.
- **Terminology and authority.** Use the project glossary, including cell, belt tile, customer seat, Prepare, Preview mode, edit mode, following range, Workshop, coins, and salvage. A catch is completed capture, not a harpoon hit. The current wireframe and later explicit user revisions govern UI; the doodle catalog governs finished visual treatment within that UI. Retain the gameplay contracts below when a presentation fixture omits their simulation. The four ADRs retain one persistent restaurant, the accepted core and expedition baselines, and separate economies.
- **Persistent restaurant.** Begin gameplay with a small rectangular workable area, a short operating open belt, one chef, and a few customer seats. The current floor-plan reference spans three connected portrait-width panels with a finished left wall: the starter panel is usable, while neighboring garbage patches are cleared gradually. Buying predefined neighboring patches permanently clears their cells. Expansion order is a player choice; cleared area, rather than remaining empty cells, determines arrivals and staff capacity. Keep this one persistent restaurant. The wireframe omits service decoration for layout review; its empty drawing is not the gameplay starting state.
- **One-cell occupancy and access.** Each placed belt tile, customer seat, and assigned chef occupies a distinct cell. A chef's blackboard stays within that footprint. Seats and chefs face exactly one adjacent belt tile; rotating them changes that connection. Customers use connected empty-floor aisles, choose reachable free seats using nearby dishes and chef blackboards, and pass or yield automatically. Disconnected chefs and seats are allowed and express inactivity through animation rather than error badges.
- **Belt topology and backpressure.** Belts connect through tile centers, may turn, and support open paths, closed loops, and separate routes. Each tile has at most one incoming and one outgoing connection; no splits or merges. Each tile holds one dish, including an open endpoint. Uneaten dishes remain in place. Blocking is local: a dish advances into a free next tile or a tile vacated in the same step. Full loops may circulate simultaneously. Customers can consume from moving or stopped facing tiles, freeing upstream capacity.
- **Automatic service and sales.** Customers have an internally variable fullness state without a displayed appetite meter, remaining-dish count, or fixed three-dish visit. Eating duration is broadly similar across recipes. Customers without food eventually become restless and leave without a fine or lasting reputation penalty. Revenue comes from dishes sold, with no extra customer-turnover payout. Each completed consumed dish credits its fixed price, updates coins, and shows a small local coin-icon pop without an amount label. Do not display an earning-rate metric.
- **First-dish preference.** Each visit gets one preference cycle. Show the preferred dish in a thought bubble with a circular preference timer. A matching first dish changes the bubble to a happy face, pairs it with a short body bounce, then fades it. On expiry, accept the next available dish at the facing tile. After the first dish, accept subsequent dishes without another preference cycle. Preference is flexible rather than a required order.
- **Chef production.** Each chef continually prepares one selected recipe. They may hold one completed dish while waiting for their loading point and begin the next after loading; no stockpile. Recipe inspection preserves unfinished preparation and held food. An actual recipe change resets unfinished preparation and replaces any held dish immediately; existing belt dishes remain unchanged. Update the blackboard immediately and briefly shake it when the picker closes to the restaurant.
- **Recipe data and eligibility.** Discoveries unlock recipes restaurant-wide. Quality tiers are Wood, Steel, Copper, Silver, and Gold, with shared starting chef-level thresholds of 1, 3, 5, 8, and 12 respectively. A chef can prepare discovered recipes at or below their highest unlocked tier; their capability ceiling can prevent reaching later tiers. These thresholds are initial balance targets. Every higher tier's fixed-price band lies entirely above every lower tier's band. Chef quality does not multiply a recipe's price. Preparation time varies independently of tier and price, including within a tier.
- **Recipe picker composition.** Use the wireframe's large portrait dialog over the restaurant, with a light scrim, chef-name/level header without a tier suffix, scrolling two-column catalog and fixed lower details/Prepare area. Tier boards use distinguishable material textures with accessible tier descriptions. Discovered tiles show dish artwork, name and fixed coin price; undiscovered tiles show engraved silhouettes without prices. Keep cookable recipes highest tier first, discovered ineligible recipes highest tier first in muted color, then undiscovered silhouettes, without visible section headings or tier labels.
- **Recipe picker states.** Open an existing chef's picker on their current recipe without changing production. A chef without a recipe starts with no selection; Prepare is unavailable until a cookable choice is made. Current and inspected tiles have distinct outlines readable together. Inspection updates fixed details without moving them during catalog scrolling. Details show fixed price and effective preparation time for this chef, excluding loading waits and omitting base time, on the required material board. Show the required chef level; keep tier information accessible rather than as redundant tier text. Ineligible details show a dash for time and disabled Prepare. The current recipe shows noninteractive Preparing. Prepare on another eligible recipe changes ongoing production and closes the picker to its originating chef context.
- **Discovery markers and dismissal.** A NEW badge remains until that recipe is inspected in a restaurant picker, including inspection by an ineligible chef. The expedition award does not clear it, and discovery does not change current chef recipes. Header close and phone system back close the picker without modifying production. Outside taps neither dismiss the dialog nor activate the restaurant beneath it.
- **Staff access and recruitment.** Staff opens a bottom tray over the visible restaurant. Show current/maximum capacity and three compact profiles per page with portrait, name and assigned/unassigned state. Put Previous/Next carets at the row's left/right edges with at least 44px targets; omit visible page numbers and announce page changes accessibly. People reuses this same roster. Tapping a profile opens separate level/stats/recipe/management details; dragging previews placement. Applicants opens a separate paper-resume popup with one candidate visible, left/right swipes or accessible controls to browse, and explicit Hire. Swiping neither hires nor rejects. Closing stats/applicants returns to the originating tray/editor and camera. Generate three deliberately varied candidates showing strengths, growth, maximum level, highest reachable tier and cost, using varied backgrounds and occupations.
- **Application lifecycle.** Offers persist until explicit Refresh. Available Refresh is free, replaces the remaining set with three candidates without confirmation or pinning, and starts a real-time cooldown that advances while closed. Hiring deducts the displayed cost, adds unassigned staff, removes only that candidate, and keeps the remaining pool and popup without refill. Disable Hire when coins are insufficient or capacity is full, retaining cost and capacity information. Header X closes browsing; omit a redundant Back to staff footer. The remaining-cooldown display choice stays deferred.
- **Chef development and retention.** Level Up in chef details shows cost, current-to-next speed and any tier unlock. Affordable taps buy one level immediately. Growth is predefined speed improvement per level; designated levels unlock whole recipe tiers. No separate speed/quality purchase tracks or cooking experience. An unaffordable action retains its preview; MAX retains final level/stats without a purchase cost. Assigned and unassigned staff count toward capacity. Unassignment retains development; named Fire confirmation permanently removes the chef without refund, but omits a separate no-refund sentence.
- **Chef placement.** Drag an unassigned portrait from the shared roster onto an empty workable cell next to a belt, in live or Edit. Actual valid placement opens its recipe picker with no selection; closing without Prepare leaves the chef idle. No separate station is required. The current wireframe drag only previews assignment while keeping the tray open; tap profile, then Recipes in stats to inspect setup. This fixture does not yet implement automatic placement-to-picker navigation or full placement validation.
- **Edit-state lifecycle.** Preview mode is live play; Edit pauses restaurant NPCs and belts. Edit and Staff sit at bottom-left in Live, with Live at top-right in Edit. People, Layout and Floor icons stack vertically at the right outside the bottom tray. Only the selected layer has a visible adjacent name, retained while collapsed. Tapping that layer collapses/reopens its tray; choosing another opens its content. Omit Service paused copy. Changes apply/persist immediately with no Save, Done, Cancel or Undo. Cross-scene navigation retains mode and tray state; it does not implicitly resume editing.
- **Selection and movement.** Tap an existing object to select it and reveal local rotation controls, then drag the selected object to move. It remains selected after release. Without a selection, dragging pans. A swipe starting outside the selection clears it and pans in that same gesture. Invalid inventory or scene-object drops return to the original position with brief rejection feedback and preserve the prior arrangement.
- **Inventory and belt path controls.** Use the shared paging rail: People has three roster profiles per page; Layout/Floor have two objects or swatches with carets at the row's left/right edges. Announce pages accessibly without visible counters. Dragging a belt tile starts a path, consumes one inventory tile, and slowly blinks four neighboring cells. A highlighted neighbor adds a tile or reuses/reorients an existing belt without consuming inventory. Reuse can replace a turn and break the previous route; old tiles remain as separate paths/disconnected segments. Tapping the starting tile closes a valid loop and ends path mode. Path mode shows remaining count, latest-tile rotate/delete and X to inventory. At zero, disable only empty-cell extensions, retaining reuse and exit. Outside highlighted-cell taps end the path. Ordinary empty-inventory feedback uses a brief nonmodal No belt tiles left toast and leaves editing usable.
- **Removal and customer recovery.** Removing purchased objects returns them to inventory without coins. Moving an occupied seat carries its customer; moving an occupied belt tile carries its dish. Removing a belt tile removes its dish without revenue. Removing a seat leaves the customer at that position with meal progress intact; they look puzzled and seek another reachable seat. Placing suitable seating on a standing customer's cell seats them immediately; other objects visibly push them to the nearest valid empty cell. If no valid empty cell exists, block overlapping placement. Trapped customers panic and wander slowly until rescued by a layout edit or direct drag.
- **Direct customer editing.** The People layer permits selecting and dragging customers while paused, preserving their visit, preference, and eating progress. Drop onto any empty floor cell or free seat, even without a walking route to it. A free-seat drop continues their visit there. Customer reactions occur after Live resumes.
- **Expansion interaction and camera.** Garbage clusters are distinct whole objects such as broken sofas, cars and shelving, with variable multi-cell footprints and separate coin costs; never repeat one debris tile across a cluster. Keep fixed-scale free finger panning, without pinch zoom, numbered shortcuts, pan carets or panel snapping. Tapping a garbage patch in either mode centers/highlights its footprint and opens the wireframe confirmation: title, patch preview, Cancel and coin-cost Clear. Omit explanatory body, separate price/dimensions, expansion numbers and redundant X. Unselected patches have no prices. Outside taps keep the card open; insufficient coins disable Clear with cost retained. Confirmation pays and clears permanently, including in Edit. Closing/cancelling leaves the camera on that patch. Shop, Workshop and Live/Edit preserve exact camera position; expedition-preparation and results returns recenter the starter restaurant floor.
- **Shop lifecycle and inventory.** The transparent floating Shop button sits below upper-left savings, stays fixed during floor panning and occupies no floor cells. Shop and Workshop share paper pages over the actual originating restaurant, with a curled top-right Return to restaurant target (at least 44px) revealing that background. Return preserves exact camera, Live/Edit mode and tray state and restores opener focus without scrolling. Use one catalog combining unlimited belt tiles/ordinary chairs and limited rotating goods, without section headings or implementation notes. Purchase targets show coin cost only, with accessible item-specific Buy names. Stock decrements on purchase and refreshes on each ended expedition return. Goods enter inventory; unaffordable offers remain visible with disabled actions. Refreshed stock sparkles on the floating Shop button and clears on entry. Live service/charge continue during shopping; Edit retains its pause.
- **Furniture scope.** Most furniture is cosmetic; a smaller subset may provide functional benefits. Benefits cannot change customer patience or dish preferences. Specific effects are remaining content design rather than implicitly promised mechanics.
- **Floor-style collection.** Floor styles are premium cosmetic rotating goods costing coins, at most one per rotation with none guaranteed. Any unowned style may appear or return from the beginning, without recent-offer protection or progression gate. Use low/middle/high price bands with higher prices less frequent and no rarity label. Show a repeated multi-cell pattern preview, name and price. Activating the cost-only purchase target unlocks unlimited application, stays in Shop, and shows a brief named unlock toast beside coins. Keep the offer as Owned until refresh; exclude owned styles from later pools.
- **Floor painting.** Floor opens with no selected style and a paginated thumbnail-only palette, newest purchased first and free original last. Omit visible style names, Owned captions and instructions; retain accessible names/selection. Selecting a style activates its brush. Tap paints one cell; drag paints crossed cells, including beneath objects, without consuming items. Keep brush/palette active between strokes. Finger panning and palette collapse preserve the selected style; edge carets belong to palette pagination and do not pan the restaurant. A brush-exit X clears the brush while retaining Floor and its palette; detailed painting/exit behavior remains to be implemented beyond the current wireframe. Compatible subtle tile variants may share a restrained slow flare; exact motion is tuning work. Styles never affect earnings, patience, preferences or access.
- **Idle economy.** There are no wages, ingredient charges, or recurring operating costs. Coins pay for restaurant construction, recruitment, chef levels, applicable cosmetics, and permanent expansion. Congestion and poor service reduce potential sales without draining savings. Recipes are earned through catches rather than bought with coins.
- **Offline restaurant progress.** Estimate aggregate throughput over the whole absence without a time cap or individual event catch-up. Use committed layout, connected chefs' recipes/effective times, prices/eligibility, belt/loading bottlenecks, reachable seats, arrivals and average preference demand. Credit the total once on reopen. Reserve a short earnings line beside/below the upper-left savings so +420 while away does not move Shop; omit a claim step and suppress zero income. Follow the catalog's rolling odometer digits as the target coin animation; the current wireframe uses fixture numerals, with motion still to implement. Restore saved customer positions, meal progress and belt dishes; resume according to saved mode. Exact throughput and saved-Edit offline income remain refinement items.
- **Passive expedition readiness.** Night service refills ship charge at a fixed sales-independent rate, initially targeting about two to three minutes. Charge advances offline up to one full battery and cannot bank multiple launches. A ready player can continue management before departure. Readiness costs neither coins nor salvage. Start consumes it; an ended expedition starts another night recharge. Active or suspended unfinished expeditions never recharge. Day/night is an in-game cadence, not a real-world time restriction.
- **Dock and preparation.** Submarine and the Workshop hut to its right share three by three floor cells in the middle panel. Workshop artwork may extend upward without covering the ship. Before access, patchwork rags cover both with small peek openings; disable their targets and omit Future access instructions. Show ship charge locally; the wireframe uses a battery with compact Ready/Charging state. A gentle headlight cue is a future visual target, without a separate HUD ready badge. Charging tap shows local time remaining; ready tap opens underwater preparation without spending charge. Start consumes readiness. Keep the Sushi Bar airlock return on preparation; its return before Start spends no charge. Shop/Workshop use their paper corner rather than this airlock.
- **Expedition route and input.** Expeditions are finite, initially targeting one to two minutes for a normal completed run. Combine authored encounter sections with varying obstacle and pickup placements. An overhead ship travels upward through a descending ocean-bed canyon on a full-screen playfield without a control footer. Touch anywhere in the playfield and drag sideways for direct relative steering. Release stops the ship's own lateral movement; retouch anchors a new drag without snapping. Do not add steering inertia, lane snapping, or automatic aiming. One finger can steer while another presses harpoon without cancelling or reanchoring steering; one-thumb release/fire/retouch remains valid.
- **Travel variety.** Long rock, reef and wreck barriers align with forward travel like train-length obstacles, forming tunnel corridors. Some span half the playfield width and extend beyond the viewport; keep a continuous steering passage. Obstacles and visible lateral currents create navigation choices. Currents can push sideways while steering remains available. Ordinary-travel boosts temporarily increase forward speed and prevent collision damage; they end before the creature encounter. Optional steer-entered portals lead to a separate safe passage where a strong current carries the ship while collecting salvage without required maneuvering, then rejoins the main route. Boosts and portals are not part of aiming or pursuit. Additional candidate encounter types are not automatically required content.
- **Expedition HUD.** The stable top row shows a compact continuous hull meter upper-left, this run's salvage icon/amount center, and pause upper-right. Hull has no numeric current/maximum; low hull changes this meter's color and gently pulses it without a screen-edge tint. A separate wide row below shows creature name and remaining resistance when the shooting window opens. Resistance starts and stays full while aiming; it measures catch effort rather than escape time. Do not replace the run's salvage with banked Workshop salvage or an earning rate.
- **Creature selection and recipe mapping.** Each catchable species maps to one distinct restaurant recipe. Select one main creature near the end. While uncaught species remain in the finite catalog, guarantee an encounter with an eligible uncaught species, not a successful catch. All uncaught species in introduced tiers have equal selection probability; older tiers remain eligible. Failed species stay eligible without a forced rematch or permanent loss. After catalog completion, retain familiar-species encounters and grant extra salvage for repeat catches.
- **Tier-pool progression.** Begin with Wood. After every ended expedition, including completion, early return, and hull depletion, make a next-tier introduction roll starting at one in three. Increase odds after misses and guarantee the next tier after three consecutive missed rolls; exact intermediate odds are tunable. This is not a fixed three-expedition schedule. If currently introduced tiers have no uncaught species, introduce the next tier before the next departure. That fallback replaces the cycle's normal roll, limiting introduction to one new tier per cycle. Continue through Steel, Copper, Silver, and Gold. A successful introduction adds an inline results notice showing tier name, material-board icon, and a short future-expedition availability message, without a separate continuation screen.
- **Harpoon opportunity.** During the limited shooting window show a fixed lower-right contextual button for all players, without a handedness setting. Harpoons fire straight toward the top from the ship's lateral position, consume no ammunition, and have a short cooldown. A fresh ready press fires once immediately; holding never repeats. Keep the button in place with muted icon and a filling circular cooldown ring, restoring its active appearance when ready. Misses may be retried within the window. Shortly before expiry, a restless creature animation warns without altering movement path or hit area. Expiry lets it escape; do not display a shooting countdown.
- **Pursuit and escape.** The first successful hook starts the creature's single pursuit attempt and enables its attacks/hazards. Ship and creature share forward speed. A narrow visible vertical following strip tracks creature lateral position, with no longitudinal limit; initially target about two ship widths. In-range following drains resistance according to reeling strength. Leaving pauses progress and starts the pursuit grace period. Return before expiry preserves progress. Tautness, color change, and fraying cable warn of danger without a return arrow or grace countdown. Grace expiry breaks the cable, ends that attempt, and awards no recipe; that creature cannot be rehooked during the encounter. A surviving ship finishes automatically after catch or escape.
- **Species behavior and fairness.** Reuse movement families such as weaving, short sideways dashes, and pauses before direction changes. Accepted attack examples include reef fragments, swirls, lateral currents, and eel electrical discharges. Use readable windups and affected-area warnings. A standard eel selects a vertical attack strip rather than tracking the submarine; warn with translucent amber fill, diagonal hatching, and a small lightning symbol. It must leave a usable safe position inside following range. Test generated hazard combinations and timings together. No direct cable-cutting or stun mechanic has been accepted.
- **Damage.** Damaging collisions reduce hull; zero hull ends the expedition as Hull depleted. During pursuit, damage also restores a small amount of resistance, setting capture back without directly breaking the cable. Preserve steering without forced sideways knockback. Impact animation and brief hit protection prevent repeated damage from the same collision. Each new departure starts with full upgraded hull, without repair costs or persistent damage.
- **Catch and award.** Zero resistance earns the catch/reward immediately and durably. End gameplay/hazards before a short automatic portrait cutscene without Skip. First discovery opens the recipe award with artwork/name, fixed price and base preparation time. Convey the minimum chef tier through the roll's material board, with accessible tier information; the Eel fixture uses copper. Omit a separate Chef requirement row. Continue opens results. A repeat goes from cutscene directly to results without another recipe award. Presentation continuation never gates reward retention.
- **Pause and resume.** Freeze the expedition behind a centered Paused card containing only its title and side-by-side Resume / Return early actions. Omit preservation/scene explanations. Outside taps keep it open. Resume consumes the menu touch, closes the card and begins a brief countdown showing only its numeral and Returning to ship... while movement/timers stay frozen. Omit Frozen scene, Resuming expedition, frozen-countdown explanation and Paused no steering labels. Preserve remaining attack, shooting, grace and capture timing without free progress/protection. A held playfield touch anchors steering to its current position when countdown ends, ignoring accumulated motion. Restart automatically; the wireframe advances its fixture explicitly through review chrome.
- **Early return and interruption.** Return early opens a centered confirmation with one short outcome sentence: Keep your haul. No completion bonus. Stay paused returns to the frozen card; confirmation ends the journey and begins recharge. An unfinished catch still earns no recipe, without another explanatory paragraph. Close/background preserves travel, hull, attacks, shooting, grace and capture state; it is not early return and spends no charge. Reopen unfinished expeditions behind Paused, requiring Resume/countdown or confirmed return.
- **Results and reward retention.** All earned salvage and discovered recipes remain after any outcome. Only completed routes earn the completion salvage bonus; finishing alone does not guarantee a recipe. Results show salvage earned during this expedition, rather than the banked Workshop balance: pickups, repeat-catch bonus when earned, completion bonus, and prominent total. Failure/early return shows zero completion bonus with a short explanation. Titles are Expedition complete, Returned early, and Hull depleted, each with a distinct generated doodle background and consistent receipt. Omit recipe recap because the first-discovery award already covers it. The sole Restaurant action returns to the bar; outcome art creates no additional rescue, repair, or reward-loss rule.
- **Concurrent restaurant earnings.** A live restaurant continues serving during an expedition and its Pause card. Expedition-time coins stay pending until restaurant return, then credit exactly once. Show a brief nonmodal credited-total message beside the updated cash balance, with a small coin icon, fading automatically and suppressed at zero. Use the same reserved earnings space as the offline cue; keep expedition-time pending coins and offline throughput distinct in accounting. The catalog's odometer motion remains a visual implementation target. Scene entrances and non-full-screen popups keep their current simulation running by default; explicit expedition Pause freezes only the dive and edit mode freezes restaurant service. Preserve separate credit records so reopening and returning cannot award the same income twice.
- **Workshop and separate currencies.** Coins and salvage never exchange. The Workshop hut beside the submarine opens the shared paper page; there is no results shortcut. Its top-right paper curl returns to the originating restaurant mode, tray and exact camera. Show Hull, Harpoon and Collector cards together with independent levels, current-to-next stat previews and salvage costs: maximum hull, reeling strength and attraction range. Live service/night charge continue while browsing/buying, subject to saved Edit pause.
- **Equipment development.** An affordable Upgrade immediately pays for exactly one level and updates salvage, level, stat preview, and next cost while staying in Workshop. Insufficient salvage disables/mutes Upgrade while preserving legible information, without a shortage popup. At a track's permanent cap, retain its full card and final level/stat, replace cost/action with MAX, and remove the next-stat arrow. Reeling upgrades drain resistance faster; Collector expands attraction range with a modest cap preserving route choices rather than multiplying pickup values. Existing encounter difficulty does not automatically scale with equipment; introduced species/patterns may be harder.
- **Equipment appearance.** At selected equipment levels, persist visible hull-panel, harpoon, or collector improvements in Workshop, dock, and expedition. Preserve the recognizable yellow silhouette, footprint, and collision size. Appearance adds no stats beyond the accepted upgrade effects and uses thresholds separate from recipe-tier introductions.
- **Guided opening.** Start with a full battery but teach basic restaurant service before introducing departures. Protect the first expedition with paused explanations and recovery opportunities so the first catch can be performed successfully. Apply normal failure and single-attempt rules afterward. The candidate sequence is sales, a simple layout edit/upgrade, steering/salvage, harpoon/pursuit, then Prepare of the discovery on an eligible chef; exact steps remain content design rather than a recurring chore gate.

## Testing Decisions

- **Agreed primary seam:** one public game-session boundary accepting player commands, elapsed time, and save/restore, and exposing observable restaurant and expedition state. The current React studio has presentation/navigation fixtures and browser checks, but no public gameplay-simulation seam. Prefer this single high-level boundary over new unit seams for belt internals, pathfinding, customer decision weights, encounter generation, or reward helpers. Use controlled clock/random inputs for deterministic scenarios without requiring production UI to expose internal details. The user confirmed this scope together with integrated UI checks for touch controls, dialogs, portrait presentation, and reopening saves.
- **What makes a good test:** create a valid player situation, perform an available action or let time pass, and assert visible/stateful consequences. Cover meaningful transitions and invariants rather than private methods, DOM structure, exact animation frames, or implementation-mirroring assertions. Compare behavior under valid layouts and actions rather than snapshotting a whole internal object graph.
- **Existing prior art:** this repository already tests engine behavior through public creation/action/time APIs and tests integrated games with Playwright. Dumpling Disco covers tutorial progression, repeated input, completion, touch cancellation, pause, and reload. Deep Salvage demonstrates an opt-in scenario/snapshot/time adapter for browser checks. Reuse tooling and conventions, not sibling gameplay code. A native delivery must exercise equivalent public simulation and actual platform UI behavior rather than claiming browser checks validate native input.
- **Restaurant simulation scenarios:** operating starter service; open endpoint retention; local blockage/recovery through stopped-tile consumption; simultaneous full-loop circulation; seat reachability and pedestrian yielding; first-preference success/expiry and subsequent acceptance; hidden variable meal completion; timeout departure without fines; fixed per-dish revenue and no recurring costs.
- **Management/edit scenarios:** recipe inspection versus actual Prepare reset; one held dish; tier/ceiling eligibility and fixed-price bands; hire/debit/capacity/refresh timing; unassign/reassign retention and confirmed firing; occupied-object moves/removals; safe displacement and blocked overlap; trapped-customer rescue preserving progress; immediate edits and Live resume; inventory accounting, zero-stock routing, existing-belt reuse, loop closure, and retained disconnected tiles; permanent expansion with cancellation and insufficient-funds behavior.
- **Shop/decor scenarios:** unlimited essentials versus limited rotating stock; exactly one rotation per ended expedition return; sparkle clearance on entry; owned-style pool exclusion and in-place Owned offer; at-most-one optional style; weighted price bands; unlimited painting beneath objects; palette order, brush exit, and finger panning that preserves the brush. Ensure cosmetics have no service or access effects.
- **Expedition scenarios:** Start-only charge consumption, cancellation before Start, capped passive/offline recharge and suspended-run non-recharge; direct relative steering; currents, boost protection, and safe bonus passages; shooting misses/cooldown/held input/expiry; one pursuit attempt; lateral-only following; grace recovery/expiry; warned safe attacks; hit protection, steering retention, and capture setbacks; catch/escape/failure completion transitions.
- **Progression/reward scenarios:** first catch unlocks exactly one species recipe at resistance zero; interrupt immediately after the catch or during cutscene, award, or results and retain it without duplicates or regression into an unfinished pursuit; preserve the completed outcome and its completion bonus and do not reroll a recorded tier introduction on reload; all outcomes retain earned rewards; completion-only bonus; repeat-catch bonus after catalog completion; equal eligible-species sampling; earlier-tier eligibility; rising tier odds and guarantee after three misses; all ended outcomes count; empty-pool fallback replaces the roll and opens at most one tier.
- **Clock/persistence scenarios:** serialize and reopen aiming, pursuit, grace, attacks, pause, and countdown-related state with timers preserved; restore behind Paused without consuming charge; menu/input handoff grants no movement, protection, or progress; real-time applicant cooldown and capped charge advance independently; uncapped layout-based offline estimate accounts for reachability, assignments, congestion, and average demand; transient restaurant state is restored; pending income and offline credit occur exactly once across repeated reopen/return. Add the saved-edit-mode offline case when its remaining policy is resolved.
- **Integrated UI checks:** real touch drag/cancel, optional simultaneous steering/fire, one-thumb reacquisition, resume-touch consumption, system-back recipe dismissal, outside-tap behavior, scrolling with fixed recipe details, current/inspected/NEW states, disabled and MAX actions, floating Shop and curled paper returns with exact camera/mode/tray preservation, preparation/results recentering, compact shared tray pagination, resume and concise confirmation copy, receipts/award flow, and live service behind supported dialogs/scenes. Check phone sizes, system insets, and rotation/wide-window portrait containment without clipping or lost controls. Check visible following and attack regions together.
- **Balance/playtesting:** evaluate recipe price/preparation curves, chef ceilings, demand, and belt/seating throughput together to detect a dominant recipe strategy. Tune following width, lateral movement, warning time, hazards, and grace together to preserve a usable safe pursuit position. Confirm ordinary run and recharge targets support short sessions. These are comparative playtests rather than brittle assertions against provisional numbers.
- **Repository validation:** for behavioral implementation changes run engine tests, the production build, and relevant integrated browser/platform checks. Preserve existing games and their phone/wide-window usability. Spec synthesis itself does not require running unchanged game tests or inventing tests for Markdown.

## Out of Scope

- Rebuilding through separate restaurant locations, prestige/reset progression, or a required complete belt loop before service.
- Repeated manual cooking/serving/clearing, required restaurant sales goals before departure, or optional intervention mechanics not decided in the grilling sessions.
- Belt splits/merges, endpoint trays, dish stacks, automatic disposal, manual tray clearing, ingredient costs, wages, recurring loss, visible fullness targets, and customer-turnover bonuses.
- Recipe mixes, cooking experience, separate chef speed/quality upgrade tracks, coin-purchased recipes, chef price multipliers, and fine-grained quality grades.
- Edit Save/Done/Cancel/Undo flows, coin refunds for removed purchased objects, landscape reflow, and pinch zoom.
- Shared or exchangeable coins/salvage, paid repairs, persistent hull damage, loss of earned expedition rewards, and multiple banked departures.
- Endless expeditions, lane controls, a control footer, auto-aim, consumable harpoon ammunition, hold-to-autofire, forced collision knockback, repeat pursuit attempts, direct cable-cut/stun attacks, and multiple main catches in one run.
- Recipe fragments/sets, target-recipe selection, pre-departure reward previews, fixed three-expedition tier schedules, equipment-gated species, or automatic difficulty scaling with purchased gear.
- Boosts/portals during the final aiming/pursuit encounter, optional additional encounter candidates treated as mandatory content, or outcome-background art treated as new mechanics.
- Equipment stat changes beyond hull, reeling strength, and pickup reach; pickup-value multipliers; floor-style gameplay effects, progression gates, rarity badges, or guaranteed floor-style stock.
- Broader commercial systems such as monetization, accounts/cloud synchronization, social features, or analytics that were not established by these artifacts. Runtime selection, native packaging/distribution, and a final product name require separately supplied delivery scope.
