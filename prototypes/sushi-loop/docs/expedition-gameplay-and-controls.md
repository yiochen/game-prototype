## Day and night loop

The restaurant owner operates the submarine. Ordinary customers remain the sushi bar's guests. The restaurant persists across sessions, with no reset of its layout, chefs, upgrades, or savings.

- Night service recharges the submarine at a fixed rate, independently of restaurant sales. Initial refill target: about two to three minutes.
- Charge advances offline during night service, up to one full battery. Further time does not bank additional launches.
- A full battery makes one expedition available. The player chooses when to launch and can keep managing the restaurant beforehand.
- In the restaurant, charge is shown only at the scene's dock, without a persistent top HUD charge badge. At full charge, the local battery stays full and the submarine's headlight gently pulses to signal readiness, without a ready badge. Tapping the charging submarine shows a brief local message with Charging and the time remaining until a full battery, keeping the player in the restaurant. This time appears on demand rather than as a persistent HUD timer. Tapping the ready submarine opens the expedition start screen; pressing Start there begins travel and consumes readiness. The dock tap itself does not launch or spend charge. The start screen has a warm sushi-bar airlock doorway with a red curtain and Sushi Bar sign instead of a back-arrow button; returning before Start preserves readiness. Exact headlight-animation colors/timing, charging-message wording/duration, and entrance placement remain open in the UI interview.
- Launch consumes readiness; every outcome returns to another night recharge. The ship does not recharge during a dive or while that dive is suspended.
- Charge does not spend restaurant coins or expedition salvage. It is a readiness resource, not an upgrade currency.
- Restaurant simulation continues in the background for the duration of the expedition, with customers, chefs, and belts progressing. Restaurant coins earned during the expedition stay pending and are credited when the player returns. The expedition Pause card freezes the dive; restaurant service continues independently. If the app is closed or backgrounded, restaurant income continues under the existing offline-income rule while expedition state is preserved.
- While the Workshop or Shop is open from live mode, restaurant chefs, belts, and customers continue service and earn restaurant coins. Passive night charging continues at the fixed rate, capped at one full battery. Scene entrances and non-full-screen popups preserve the current simulation by default. The expedition Pause card freezes the dive only; edit mode pauses restaurant service.

Charging is deliberately passive: night service need not contain a mandatory task or restaurant performance target. This provides spacing between expeditions rather than requiring a manual restaurant action before each launch. Day/night describes the in-game cycle; the short-session timing does not require waiting for real-world daylight.

Dockside charging uses the accepted dock and submarine presentation. Submarine and the compact Workshop landmark share a 3 × 3 floor-cell footprint in the middle restaurant panel, with Workshop to the submarine’s right. Before access is available, patchwork rags cover both with small peek openings and unavailable targets. Specific charging effects and day/night lighting remain to be designed.

## Journey and steering

- Expeditions are finite, with normal completed runs initially targeting one to two minutes.
- Routes vary by combining deliberately designed encounter sections and changing obstacle and pickup placements.
- An overhead submarine travels up the screen along the ocean bed into a descending canyon. The scene fills the screen without a control footer.
- Touch anywhere in the playfield and drag sideways for direct relative steering. Release stops the submarine's own sideways movement; forward travel continues.
- Touching again starts a new relative drag without snapping the ship to the finger. There is no steering-induced lateral inertia or lane snapping.
- During the shooting window, one finger can continue a steering drag while another taps the harpoon button. Firing does not cancel or reanchor that drag. Using two fingers is optional: one-thumb play can release steering, fire, and begin a new relative drag without snapping the ship. Touch handoff remains open in the UI interview.
- Environmental currents can cause lateral drift, which the player countersteers or rides toward pickups.
- The creature is the final major encounter, near the end. The final section continues until it is caught, escapes, or the ship fails. A surviving ship then finishes automatically.

The expedition HUD overlays the full-screen scene. Its top status row places the compact hull meter at upper-left, salvage collected this expedition as an icon and amount in the center, and pause at upper-right. The salvage amount shows this run's pickups, not the Workshop's banked balance or an earning rate. The creature's name and resistance bar appear in a separate wide row just below when the shooting window opens, keeping the ship-status positions stable. Exact insets, sizes, colors, and creature-bar entrance/exit presentation remain open.

## Travel encounters

Solid obstacles and varied route sections supply ordinary navigation challenges. Exact content patterns remain to be authored.

The wireframe uses obstacles elongated along forward travel, with train-length proportions like the Subway Surfers reference, to form a canyon or tunnel corridor around the ship and pickups. Their rock, reef and underwater wreck forms fit the ocean setting. Widths vary up to roughly half the portrait playfield, while an obstacle's length may extend beyond the visible screen. Preserve the overhead view and free relative lateral drag; the reference adds no runner lanes, lane snapping or jumping. These are presentation fixtures for visual scale and readable passages. They do not settle production collision shapes, obstacle frequency, damage, route difficulty or upgrade scaling; those remain authored content and playtest tuning under the accepted steering, hit-protection and safe-position rules. Shared obstacle and travel-cue renderers are used in both component examples and the expedition scene.

**Boosts:** temporarily increase forward speed and prevent collision damage while the player steers and collects salvage. They occur during ordinary travel and end before the creature encounter.

**Portals:** optional entrances reached by steering. A portal leads to a separate safe bonus passage, where a strong current carries the ship while it collects salvage without requiring maneuvering. It then rejoins the main route. Portals occur before the creature encounter, not during aiming or pursuit.

**Lateral currents:** visibly flowing water pushes the ship sideways while steering remains available. These differ from the automatic current in a bonus passage.

Moving creatures, gates, whirlpools, wreck passages, and collapsing passages remain additional content candidates; inclusion and exact rules are not assumed from the earlier brainstorm.

## Harpoon and capture

Each distinct catchable species corresponds to a distinct restaurant recipe. Until the collection is complete, every expedition guarantees a new creature whose recipe unlocks restaurant-wide. After each expedition, there is a one-in-three chance that recipes from the next tier enter the catch pool, moving from Wood toward Gold. This is a random chance, not a fixed three-expedition schedule. After a missed roll, the chance rises on subsequent expeditions and guarantees the next tier after three consecutive misses. Completion, early return, and hull depletion each count as an expedition for tier rolls. All uncaught species from previously introduced tiers remain eligible as new species enter the pool, and every eligible creature has equal selection chance. If the current tiers have no eligible uncaught species, add the next tier before the next expedition so the new-creature guarantee holds; this fallback replaces that cycle's normal post-run tier roll, so at most one new tier opens in the cycle. When a tier enters the pool, show a brief notice on the expedition results screen that recipes from that tier can now appear on future expeditions; the notice layout is inline on the normal results screen; its exact content remains open. Exact intermediate odds remain open. Recipe tiers are Wood, Steel, Copper, Silver, and Gold. Shared starting chef-level thresholds are Wood 1, Steel 3, Copper 5, Silver 8, and Gold 12. A chef whose capability ceiling is below a threshold can never reach that tier. Recipe tiles are rendered as boards made from their tier's material, with distinct color and texture; undiscovered dishes appear engraved as silhouettes, and discovered dishes appear as full-color illustrations. Rewards are not previewed before departure. After a successful catch, automatically play a full-screen portrait cutscene lasting only a few seconds, without a Skip button, then automatically show the award dialog. For first-time discoveries, the dedicated recipe reveal shows recipe art/name, fixed price per dish and base preparation time. Its roll/board uses the required chef tier’s material, such as copper for the Eel example, without a visible Chef requirement row; preserve tier information in an accessible description. Continue opens expedition results. The catch and reward are already earned when resistance reaches zero; neither the cutscene nor the dialog gates their retention. Active play has ended, with no hazards continuing behind the cutscene or award dialog. Ordinary repeat catches retain their results flow without a new-recipe reveal. Chef thresholds remain open to balance tuning, as do material details, cutscene content, and timing within the few-second target; no specific second count has been fixed.

On returning to the restaurant, newly unlocked recipes have a small NEW badge in the chef's recipe picker until inspected there. The picker is a large portrait dialog over the restaurant with a light scrim that keeps the live scene visible, with an upper scrolling two-column recipe-tile catalog and fixed lower recipe details panel. On opening, the panel shows the selected chef's current recipe without changing live service. Keep catalog ordering without visible section titles: recipes the chef can cook, sorted from highest to lowest tier; discovered recipes above the chef's tier, sorted from highest to lowest and shown in muted color; then undiscovered recipes shown as silhouettes engraved on their tier-material boards. Each tile is a board made from its tier's material. Discovered tiles show dish artwork, name and fixed coin selling price, with a NEW marker where applicable. Material textures convey tier without a visible tier label, and accessible descriptions retain tier information. Undiscovered silhouettes reveal no price. The header shows the chef’s name and level without a tier suffix. Outline the current recipe tile and use a different outline for the tile selected for inspection; keep both outlines distinguishable when the same tile has both states. The expedition award dialog does not clear the NEW marker. Tapping a discovered recipe updates the fixed lower panel with its artwork, fixed price and preparation time on a board made from its required tier’s material, without redundant tier text. Retain accessible tier information and show applicable required chef level; scrolling leaves the panel in place. When the selected recipe is already the chef's current recipe, the panel shows a noninteractive Preparing status instead of Prepare. Selecting a different eligible recipe restores Prepare; choosing it closes the picker to its originating chef context. The chef’s blackboard updates immediately and briefly shakes when the restaurant is visible again. The picker can also be closed with its header icon or the phone's system back gesture, preserving the chef's current recipe and cooking state. Taps outside the dialog do not dismiss it or trigger underlying restaurant actions. For cookable recipes, this panel shows only effective preparation time adjusted for the chef's current speed, excluding belt-loading waits and omitting base time. The expedition award retains its separate recipe-wide base-time fact. Discovered recipes above this chef's tier remain inspectable with their material board, accessible tier description and required level, but Prepare is disabled and preparation time shows a dash (—) while the chef is ineligible. Inspection clears the NEW marker without requiring recipe selection or cooking, and existing chef recipes remain intact until the player changes them. Service continues throughout. Tier thresholds, material treatment, exact card/panel dimensions and artwork/insets, and blackboard-shake timing/amplitude remain open in the UI interview.

1. One main creature appears near the journey's end. Until the finite catalog is complete, it is guaranteed to be an uncaught species from the tiers currently introduced by expedition milestones. Uncaught species from earlier tiers remain eligible. After each expedition, a one-in-three chance adds the next tier to this pool; the chance rises after each missed roll and guarantees expansion after three consecutive misses. Completion, early return, and hull depletion each count as an expedition for tier rolls.
2. A contextual floating harpoon button appears when a catchable creature is nearby. The UI interview subsequently accepted anchoring it in the lower-right corner during the shooting window, without a footer. This position applies to all players, with no left-side placement setting. The game is locked to portrait; exact insets and button size remain open. Steering remains available without a dedicated control deck. See [the UI interview](./ui-design-interview.md).
3. Harpoons fire straight toward the top from the ship's lateral position. Shots do not aim automatically, consume no ammunition, and have a short cooldown. A fresh button press fires immediately when ready; holding does not repeat. Another shot requires another press after cooldown. During cooldown, the button stays in place with a muted harpoon icon and a circular ring filling around it; its active appearance returns when ready. There is no numeric timer or separate cooldown HUD element. Exact cooldown duration, colors, and animation treatment remain open.
4. Missed shots may be retried during the limited shooting window. Waiting too long, missing repeatedly, or not shooting lets the creature swim away. Shortly before expiry, a visibly restless creature animation, such as quicker fin movements and extra bubbles, warns of its departure. This warning does not change its movement path or hit area. At expiry, it swims away; there is no shooting-window countdown in the HUD. Exact warning duration and animation remain open.
5. A successful hit starts that creature's single pursuit attempt. Its attacks and hazard creation begin after hooking, not while the initial shot is being lined up.
6. Staying in following range drains creature resistance. The UI interview refined this to a narrow, visible vertical strip tracking the creature's horizontal position, with no top or bottom boundary or forward-distance check. During pursuit, ship and creature advance at the same forward speed; the player controls lateral alignment. The initial width target is about two submarine widths, with exact tuning left to playtesting. Standard attacks must still leave a usable safe position within it.
7. Leaving range pauses progress and starts a grace period. Returning promptly preserves progress. The cable becomes taut, changes color, and frays as the window runs out. The UI interview accepted these cable warnings and the visible strip without a return arrow or explicit grace countdown.
8. Grace-period expiry breaks the cable. The creature escapes without its recipe, and that encounter cannot be rehooked to retry pursuit.
9. Empty creature resistance completes the catch. The boss-style bar displays remaining resistance and the creature's name in a wide row below the top status row. It appears full when the shooting window opens and stays full while aiming. Harpoon shots themselves do not drain resistance; after hooking, following within range drains it toward capture. The bar does not represent time until escape.

## Creature behaviors and attacks

Species differ through reusable lateral movement families, such as smooth weaving, short sideways dashes, or pauses before sideways direction changes. Their pursuit forward speed matches the ship's. Some also create hazards or attack: reef breaking into scattered fragments, swirls, lateral currents, and eel electrical discharges are accepted examples whose exact patterns remain to be authored.

Attacks use affected-area warnings and readable windups. For the standard eel discharge, the creature selects a vertical strip and then shoots through it; it does not target or track the submarine. The UI interview accepted translucent amber fill, diagonal hatching, and a small lightning symbol for its pre-discharge warning. The strip leaves a safe position within following range, so avoiding that strip alone does not require abandoning pursuit.

Generated hazard combinations, warning durations, and attack scheduling must be tested alongside following range and grace timing. No additional direct cable-cutting or stun effect has been accepted.

## Damage, outcomes, and interruptions

- Damaging hits reduce hull durability. At zero hull, the expedition fails.
- The UI interview accepted a compact continuous hull meter with a submarine icon at upper-left in the top status row, without numeric current/maximum durability. It is distinct from the creature-resistance bar below that row. While hull is low, the meter takes on a warning color and gently pulses; this warning stays at the meter without a screen-edge tint. Exact insets, sizes, colors, low-hull threshold, pulse timing, and intensity remain open.
- During pursuit, hits also set capture progress back slightly, restoring some creature resistance. They do not directly break the cable.
- Hits preserve lateral steering without forced sideways knockback. Hit animation and brief protection communicate impact and prevent repeated damage from one collision.
- Every new expedition starts with full upgraded hull durability. There is no repair charge or persistent hull damage.
- All earned salvage and unlocked recipes remain after failure or early return. An unfinished pursuit earns no recipe.
- Automatic journey completion grants a salvage bonus. Completion itself does not award a guaranteed recipe.
- Players may deliberately return early: keep earned rewards, forgo the completion bonus, and begin another night recharge.
- Return early from the pause card opens a centered confirmation with brief outcome text: Keep your haul. No completion bonus. Stay paused restores the Paused card without resuming the world; a second Return early tap ends the journey and opens results. The concise copy preserves the rules that unfinished pursuits earn no recipe, earned rewards remain retained and ending the journey begins recharge.
- Use the current centered Paused card over the frozen expedition scene, with only its Paused title and side-by-side Resume and Return early actions. Omit Expedition preserved and explanatory frozen-world copy. Outside taps leave the card open and the expedition frozen; only Resume starts the countdown. Resume dismisses the card and shows only a countdown numeral and Returning to ship...; omit Frozen scene and Paused · no steering labels. The entire expedition stays frozen until the countdown ends, then play restarts automatically. Remaining attack, shooting, and pursuit timers are preserved, with no extra protection or catch progress. The Resume-button touch is consumed by the menu. A separate playfield touch may be held ready during the countdown; at its completion, steering anchors to the finger's current position. Subsequent dragging moves the ship without snapping or accumulated countdown movement. Exact countdown duration remains open.
- Closing or backgrounding the app pauses and preserves the journey, including travel, attacks, shooting windows, grace timing, and capture state. It does not count as an early return or begin charging.
- Reopening an unfinished expedition restores that state behind the same centered Paused card. Resume begins the accepted countdown; reopening itself does not restart the journey. Return early remains available through its confirmation. No new charge is spent, and saved hull and encounter timers are preserved.

Expedition results show a salvage receipt: pickups, a repeat-catch bonus when earned, and the completion bonus, followed by a prominent total earned. After any expedition outcome with restaurant earnings, briefly show the total coins earned during the expedition beside the restaurant cash balance. Suppress the notice when the total is zero. The text fades out automatically, requires no dismissal, and does not pause service. When the expedition fails or ends early, show the completion bonus as zero with a short explanation. The receipt shows this expedition's earned salvage, not the banked balance or an earning rate. Previously collected rewards remain retained; the dedicated first-catch recipe award dialog already shows the recipe and its accepted facts. Continue from that dialog opens receipt-only results, without another recipe recap. The single Restaurant action then returns to the sushi bar. Titles identify the outcome: Expedition complete for a finished route, Returned early for a voluntary return, and Hull depleted when hull reaches zero. Each has a different image-generated background behind the consistent receipt and Restaurant action. These backgrounds do not introduce repair, rescue, permanent-damage, or lost-reward mechanics. Exact receipt/background styling remains open.

Both the results' Restaurant action and the preparation screen's Sushi Bar doorway recenter the restaurant view on the cleared floor at the accepted fixed scale, with normal swipe panning available afterward. Workshop and Shop instead preserve the originating restaurant camera position and live/edit mode. Preparation returns preserve readiness; post-run returns retain the accepted recharge and reward rules.

## Progression and upgrades

Restaurant coins fund restaurant development; salvage funds permanent submarine upgrades. The currencies do not exchange. Recipes connect expedition success to restaurant income; see [the economy decision](./adr/0003-separate-restaurant-and-expedition-economies.md).

The submarine improvement area is called **Workshop**. Expedition results retain their single Restaurant continuation, with no Workshop shortcut. Access Workshop afterward through the compact physical hut/sign to the docked submarine’s right. Inside, Workshop and Shop share a paper page over the actual originating restaurant. A curled top-right corner reveals that restaurant and provides an accessible Return to restaurant target of at least 44 × 44 px, replacing the old Sushi Bar doorway. Returning retains purchased upgrades, exact camera position, live/edit mode and open/collapsed layer state, and restores focus to the opener without scrolling. Live restaurant chefs, belts and customers continue service and earn coins while Workshop is open; entering from Edit retains the restaurant pause. Night charging continues at its fixed rate, capped at one full battery. Return shows current live service rather than restoring earlier transient customer/dish activity. The corner grows from 48 px to 72 px over 160 ms on hover or press; current wireframe navigation is immediate. A 200 ms paper fade is proposed, with direct restoration under reduced motion. Expedition preparation/results retain their separate restaurant-floor recentering rule.

Workshop shows the three Hull, Harpoon, and Collector upgrade cards together, with their current level, a current-to-next stat preview, and salvage cost. The previews show maximum hull, reeling strength, and pickup reach. An affordable Upgrade tap immediately buys exactly one level without confirmation, deducts its salvage cost, and updates the balance, level, stat preview, and next cost while keeping the player in the Workshop. If salvage is insufficient, Upgrade is muted and disabled, with the cost, current level, and stat preview still legible. It opens no shortage message when tapped. At the track's maximum level, retain the full card in place with its final level and stat, replace Upgrade and cost with a noninteractive MAX badge, and remove the next-stat arrow. Exact units, gains, costs, caps, feedback animation, and visual placement remain open.

At a few selected equipment levels, visible hull-panel, harpoon, or collector improvements appear on the submarine. They persist in the Workshop, restaurant dock, and expedition while preserving its recognizable yellow silhouette, footprint, and collision size. They add no equipment effects beyond the accepted stats. Exact appearance thresholds and artwork remain open; these thresholds are separate from capture milestones that unlock species.

Expedition access is the visibly docked yellow submarine beside the restaurant, rather than a toolbar button, with a charge indicator local to the dock. At full charge, its headlight gently pulses while the battery remains full; no ready badge is added. A ready dock tap opens a simple underwater expedition start screen with the ship, Start, and a warm sushi-bar airlock return entrance, without an equipment summary or reward preview. Start begins the journey and consumes readiness. While charging, a dock tap shows a brief local message with Charging and the time remaining until full, keeping the player in the restaurant. Exact entry placement, headlight-animation colors/timing, and charging-message wording/duration remain open.

- New species enter a progression-based pool through capture milestones. There is a one-in-three starting chance per expedition of adding the next tier before eligible uncaught species run out; uncaught species from earlier tiers remain eligible. Odds rise after misses and guarantee expansion after three consecutive misses. A successful tier roll gets a brief notice on expedition results; exact intermediate odds and tier-unlock notice content remain open.
- Each expedition chooses randomly from eligible uncaught species. A failed species stays eligible; there is no forced rematch or permanent loss.
- The creature catalog is finite. After completion, expeditions retain familiar-species encounters; successful repeat catches award extra salvage.
- Upgrade tracks are separate and permanently capped. Priorities are hull/survival, harpoon reeling strength, and salvage collection equipment.
- Reeling upgrades drain resistance faster while within following range.
- Collection upgrades increase salvage attraction range, with a modest cap that preserves route choices; they do not multiply pickup values.
- Existing encounter difficulty does not scale upward with ship upgrades. Stronger gear makes existing challenges easier; milestones introduce harder species and patterns.

## Guided opening

Start with a full battery, but first guide the player through basic restaurant operation before introducing the expedition. The first expedition is a protected lesson: pause for explanations and permit recovery from mistakes so the player can perform and complete the first catch. Normal failure and one-chance rules apply afterward.

A proposed teaching sequence is cooking/customer sales, a simple layout edit and upgrade, steering and salvage, harpooning and pursuit, then returning to choose Prepare for the discovered recipe on a suitable chef. Exact steps remain to be designed; this is not a new mandatory chore loop.
